## What the screen says, and why it is derived rather than written

**Everything comes off the `ScheduleRangeView`.** The three windows carry the wording and the send
time `SENDING_PREFERENCE_OPTIONS` publishes; the grouping, the holds, the exceptions, the counts and
the disposition are Task 12's. This file chooses words and layout. The one thing it formats itself is
the AWST wall-clock time of a contact that sends at **no** approved time — the three windows publish
their own `sendTime` and the screen renders those.

**One read feeds the whole screen.** `buildScheduleRange` is asked for the seven-day strip, and the
day open beneath it is one of the days that call returned. There is deliberately no second call for
"the selected day": a strip that disagreed with the day under it is exactly the quiet inconsistency a
schedule cannot afford, and Task 12 built the range entry point so that it could not happen.

### Ruling [126] — named by the time, never as an act

The group is headed **"Not at an approved send time"** and each row states the time it sends at. It
is not called "moved", it does not invent a fourth window, and it does not hide the contact among the
three. The corrected reasoning is written into the file so it cannot be re-argued from the group's
existence alone: a deliberate move is the only way to REACH an off-window time, but the converse is
false — a morning plan's contact moved to 2:00 pm lands silently inside the afternoon window and
nothing in `PlanRecord` records that a move happened — so "moved" would be true of everything in the
group and would miss every moved contact that landed on an approved hour.

**I could not find an honest way to distinguish a moved contact from an unmoved one, and did not
try to derive one from the window.** `PlanRecord` carries `planned.sendAt` and `planned.calendarDay`
and no history; `rescheduleContact` writes the new instant over the old. The audit trail records the
write, but that is a different read on a different surface and is not what a schedule row has. So the
screen states what it can attest and nothing more. Mutation **M8** pins this: it leaves the heading
and the time intact and only puts the word "moved" into the group's description, and the suite goes
red on that alone.

### The four states that must not collapse

1. **Every contact stopped, against none planned.** A stopped day renders its contacts, its numbers
   and, per contact, why it will not be sent; the statement reads "No contact on this day will be
   sent at all." A day with nothing on it renders an empty state and no numbers at all. M2 and M9
   both falsify this pair.
2. **A held plan is not an exception, and is not a quiet day.** A draft plan whose first contact day
   has arrived is reported by the read as `held`, stays out of the named-exceptions panel — Task 12's
   judgement, rendered rather than re-decided — and is stated **above the day's windows** as its own
   automated state: "Plan not started", with why ("a discharged patient is receiving nothing while
   the plan record looks complete") and what would change it ("starting the plan, from that patient's
   record"). Each held row also names the hold and points at that statement.

   The notice is derived from the **entries**, not from `counts.held`, because the count says how
   many are held and not by what, and a coordinator's next move differs completely between a plan
   nobody started and a plan somebody paused. Only contacts that would otherwise go out are
   considered: `planHold` is reported on a sent contact too, and telling a reader that an ended plan
   is holding a message it already sent would name the wrong reason for the same silence. **That
   guard was unproven until I wrote an assertion for it** — see "What running the mutations changed".

3. **`nothingDue` is three days, and `counts` says which.** With nothing due, `alreadySent`, `held`
   and `willNotBeSent` partition the day exactly, so the statement names whichever parts are present
   and says "every" or "some" from the same numbers. Three seeded days — one delivered, one draft,
   one withdrawn — report the same disposition and produce three different statements. M1 and M2
   falsify it.
4. **An empty day you chose, against an empty stretch of days.** A day with nothing on it inside a
   strip that holds contacts elsewhere gets the `filtered` shape: it names the day, says other days
   above do hold contacts, and offers a link to one of them — a remedy that is a real control
   directly above (Ruling 93). A strip with nothing anywhere in it says something else and offers no
   such link, because sending a clinician back to the strip there would send them hunting through
   days that are all equally empty. M9 falsifies it.

**Where the "filter" in §4.4 lands on this screen, stated because it is a judgement.** The approved
design gives this screen no filter, and I did not add one. The narrowing a coordinator actually
performs here is choosing a day, so that is what the `filtered` kind is used for. If the owner wants
a real filter later — "only what is still to go out" is the obvious one, and `entry.isDue` already
carries it — the empty-state branch it needs is already the right shape.

### Counts are a data readout, never prose

Ruling 94. The day's numbers are a `<dl>` sitting directly above the lists they count; the prose
beside it carries no number at all. The first four rows partition the day; **named exceptions cut
across that partition** — a missed contact will not be sent, and a contact whose transport receipt
never arrived has already been sent, and both are exceptions — so that row is separated from the four
and labelled as the panel it matches rather than as another part of the split.

Nothing on the screen or in this report states how many contacts a plan has.

## The one domain addition, and why it is not on the screen

The day strip has to name the days either side of the one being looked at, which is arithmetic over
an **AWST calendar day**. `schedule-view.ts` already does this privately (`calendarDaysFrom`), and
the brief froze that module. The choice was therefore between writing the arithmetic on the screen —
a second place in the codebase that knows how to step an AWST day, on the surface that tells a
coordinator when a discharged patient hears from the service — or putting it where AWST calendar days
already live.

I put it in `clock.ts` as `awstCalendarDayOffset`, and it steps from **midday** rather than midnight
for the reason `schedule-view.ts` records: midday is the furthest point from either day boundary, so
adding whole days cannot cross one by accident.

**This leaves a duplication I was not allowed to resolve.** `schedule-view.ts`'s `calendarDaysFrom`
is the same arithmetic, and one of the two should go. It is stated in both files and reported here
rather than fixed. **If the controller would rather the helper had not been added at all, reversing
it means putting `middayOf`-style arithmetic on the page, which I think is the worse of the two.**

## What this screen deliberately does NOT read

**No patient name.** The caseload reads `listPatientNames` (Ruling 91) because recognising your own
patients is the whole job of a caseload. A schedule answers a different question — what does the
service send today, and what needs a person — and it is a working view a coordinator returns to
through the day. Folding an identity-bearing read into it would write a `patientName` row into the
access trail every time somebody glanced at a day, which is the opposite of what that row is for: it
exists so "who read patients' names, and when" has a short, meaningful answer. Every row is headed by
the synthetic identifier and links to the patient record, which holds the name and is audited for
reading it.

**This is a difference between the approved design and what is built**, recorded rather than resolved:
the mockup's schedule shows patient names. It is a one-prop change if the owner wants them — the page
would add the read and the capability exactly as the caseload does — and it would then also need the
"names are not shown in this role" notice, which `patients-directory.tsx` currently owns privately and
whose own comment says it should move to `workspace/` on its second use. **I did not read names, so I
did not move it.**

## The access trail

`{ search, contactSchedule, "<from>:<to>" }` — byte for byte the identity
`src/app/api/caring-contacts/schedule/route.ts` records for the same question, even though the store
read underneath is `listPlans`. Recording it as `plan` would be defensible by provenance and wrong by
meaning: the trail's query surface has no `objectId` filter, so a caseload read and a schedule read
sharing a member would become one undifferentiated stream and "which days did this clinician look at"
would stop being answerable. Ruling 46, Ruling [125]. M16 falsifies it.

The service-state read is the usual `{ administrative, serviceState, "service" }`, required on every
screen (Ruling 56) and passed to the shell as a state that was actually read.

### Two assertions that could not have failed, found by asking

Both were written by me and both were caught before the ledger, by asking of each assertion whether
it could go red rather than whether it passed:

- The strip's count check read the whole link's text, and `"Mon 31 Aug"` already contains a `"1"` — so

**My driver did not already do this.** The line is added:

```js
if (expected === before) {

}
```

Round 2's description of the check is corrected in place above rather than left to be read as complete.
